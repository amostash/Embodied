New Kashan
Concept · Architectural · Development Brief

New Kashan
A 21st-Century Persian Village

A real community rooted in Persian spatial intelligence and cultural continuity — a prototype for a democratic, sustainable, future-facing Iran, and a place people genuinely want to live, visit, learn and gather.

Santa Fe–Albuquerque Corridor, NM ~25 Dwellings Net-Zero Retreat & Living Lab
01 · Executive Vision

Not a replica. A continuation.

New Kashan is a small, intentional village in the American Southwest: a contemporary Persian settlement rather than a replica, theme park, or nostalgic reconstruction. Its architecture, landscape, governance and daily life draw from durable Iranian traditions — courtyard urbanism, shade, gardens, water, hospitality, craftsmanship, poetry, food and civic gathering — while incorporating modern ecological systems, democratic governance, appropriate technology and a pluralistic social culture.

"If the architectural and civic intelligence of Kashan had evolved continuously into the 21st century, what might it become?"

Four levels at once

What the village has to be, simultaneously

  • A home for a small permanent community
  • A retreat and hospitality destination
  • A cultural bridge presenting a sophisticated, non-kitschy vision of Iran
  • A practical testbed for technologies and institutions relevant to a future free and sustainable Iran

Why Kashan, specifically

Domestic, civic, human-scaled

  • Climate intelligence — thick walls, thermal mass, shade, courtyards, passive cooling
  • Spatial intelligence — private-to-public gradients, intimate lanes, internal gardens
  • Cultural intelligence — hospitality, tea, food, poetry, music, craft, conversation
  • Symbolic value — a recognizable Iranian identity without reducing Persian culture to ornament
A visitor should not think, "This is a reproduction of old Persia." They should think, "This feels deeply Persian — and like a place the future could actually inhabit." New Kashan — success criterion
What makes this different from a themed development

Kashan avoids monumental imperial imagery — no domes borrowed from mosque architecture, no ornament applied for its own sake. The reference stays domestic and civic: merchant courtyard houses, craft traditions, an urban scale that translates honestly into a small contemporary settlement. That restraint is what allows the project to be extended, in this brief, into territory the original inspiration never had to address — a net-zero energy budget, a written charter, a fellowship pipeline for founders, a shared calendar of festivals that gives the architecture something to be used for. Persian identity here is treated as a living, evolving civilization, not a museum piece.

02 · Location Strategy

High desert, chosen on purpose

The leading hypothesis is the Santa Fe–Albuquerque corridor, on land outside incorporated Santa Fe, within roughly an hour of a major airport and meaningful urban services. The region offers an unusually compatible combination of high-desert landscape, existing earthen-building traditions, cultural tourism, artistic identity, retreat infrastructure and a strong sustainability ethos.

Santa Fe County regulates development through its Sustainable Land Development Code (SLDC), using planned-development tools to support coherent community design, mixed uses, walkability, infrastructure efficiency and preservation of natural and cultural resources. That regulatory vocabulary is unusually aligned with the concept — though feasibility depends entirely on the zoning, water, wastewater, access and development rights of a specific parcel.

Site selection criteria

Access

  • 30–60 min from Albuquerque Sunport or another well-connected airport
  • 20–45 min from a substantial town for hospitals, groceries, contractors, employment
  • Reliable legal and emergency-vehicle access

Buildable

  • Enough acreage for 8–12 dwellings initially, expandable toward ~25
  • Entitlement pathway for clustered housing + hospitality/retreat + limited commercial uses
  • Good solar exposure, terrain suited to passive-solar orientation

Resource & risk

  • A credible water strategy: community water, transferable rights, or viable domestic supply
  • Wastewater feasibility — community system, sewer, or engineered decentralized treatment
  • No fatal floodplain, wildfire, archaeological, slope or habitat constraints

Landscape character should remain substantially native rather than being remade into an artificial Persian set — with sufficient separation from neighbors to support retreats and events, while staying socially connected to the region.

03 · Architectural Language

Two earthen traditions, one wall

The architecture should feel inevitable in the landscape. Persian references arise from proportion, sequence, material, climate response and social use — never applied decoration. New Mexico makes this credible rather than metaphorical: adobe, compressed earth block and rammed earth are not an aesthetic import here, they are already addressed within the state's adopted construction-code framework.

Core spatial principles
  • Courtyard-first planning — homes organize around protected outdoor rooms, not front lawns
  • A clear gradient from public, to semi-public, to intimate, to private space
  • Narrow, shaded pedestrian passages opening occasionally into courtyards and plazas
  • Human scale — mostly one story, selectively two where views, density or civic emphasis justify it
  • Thick, tactile wall construction that visually and thermally evokes mass
  • Deep window reveals, shade structures, arcades, carefully framed views
  • Roofs used pragmatically — solar, terraces, rain capture, night-sky experience — not decorative silhouettes
  • Restrained palette: earth-toned plaster, earthen materials, timber, stone, metal and tile used sparingly
The adobe / khesht kinship

Persian courtyard architecture and Southwestern Pueblo adobe architecture are, in a real sense, cousins rather than borrowed neighbors — both are engineering responses, arrived at independently, to the same problem: how to live well in an arid, high-thermal-swing climate without imported energy. Persian builders call sun-dried mud brick khesht; Pueblo and Hispano builders in New Mexico call the same material adobe. Both traditions converge on thick mass walls, small deliberate openings, courtyards as outdoor rooms, and roofs as usable surface. New Kashan's architectural brief is to let that kinship do the design work — a windcatcher-inspired stack-ventilation chimney is not a "Persian motif" bolted onto a New Mexican house, it is the same physics the Yazd badgir and the Pueblo's oriented massing both discovered on their own.

Earthen construction workstream

Adobe

Traditional sun-dried brick, thick thermal mass, deep vernacular precedent on-site in New Mexico. Labor-intensive; excellent thermal performance for this climate.

Compressed earth block

Mechanically pressed rather than sun-dried — faster, more dimensionally consistent, still visually and thermally continuous with adobe.

Rammed earth / hybrid

Higher upfront cost, strong structural and moisture performance; can be paired with insulated cores for code-driven efficiency targets.

The architectural team should investigate all three, plus hybrid wall systems, against structural, moisture, insulation, labor and cost realities — with reference to New Mexico's dedicated Earthen Building Materials Code. Explicitly avoided: faux-historic arches, excessive turquoise ornament, decorative domes, and imported "Persian" motifs used without spatial purpose. No Disneyland Persia.

04 · Landscape

A Persian garden, honestly reinterpreted

The landscape references the Persian idea of the garden as a cultivated microclimate and social refuge — but does not impose a water-intensive garden on the high desert. The modern interpretation is a disciplined choreography of shade, fragrance, edible planting, sound, seasonal color, and precious water.

Water as precious, not decorative

  • The chahar-bagh fourfold geometry organizes one or two important civic gardens — not repeated everywhere as a motif
  • Visible water stays concentrated: short rills, small basins, recirculating elements at the social heart, rather than large ornamental pools
  • Stormwater harvesting made visible and beautiful — swales, basins and planted channels as part of the ecological narrative

Planting that belongs to the desert

  • Native and drought-adapted species prioritized; Persian/Mediterranean analogues only where ecologically sensible
  • Microclimates created with walls, pergolas, vines, deciduous shade and courtyard orientation
  • Large areas of existing high-desert landscape preserved as the setting for the settlement itself
05 · Net-Zero & Systems

Sustainability as infrastructure, not branding

The village aims for high environmental performance without turning sustainability into visual gadgetry. Every system should be measurable, maintainable, and capable of serving as a demonstration for arid-region development elsewhere — including, eventually, arid regions of Iran itself.

Measure performance. Design for maintainability. No sustainability theater.

The three net-zero ledgers

Energy

Net-zero / net-positive

  • Solar microgrid with battery storage, grid-connected where practical for resilience
  • All-electric or near-all-electric buildings, high-efficiency heat pumps
  • Passive-solar design, thermal mass, exterior shading, night ventilation appropriate to climate
  • Community microgrid tested for resilience during grid outages

Water

Net-zero draw where feasible

  • Rainwater catchment integrated from day one — Santa Fe County requires catchment on new residential and nonresidential builds
  • Greywater reuse where legally and technically feasible
  • Metering at dwelling and community level, leak detection, transparent water budgets
  • Low-water fixtures and landscaping; alternative sanitation only if code-compliant

Carbon

Embodied + operational

  • Earthen construction carries inherently low embodied carbon versus imported materials
  • Low-carbon, local materials chosen for repairability and long design life
  • Shared mobility, EV charging, bicycles, perimeter parking to minimize internal roads and paving
  • Community-scale food production focused on high-value, climate-appropriate crops — not a self-sufficiency claim
Living-laboratory instrumentation

A portion of the project explicitly tests systems relevant to arid settlements and future reconstruction — not speculative technology for its own sake, but technologies evaluated in ordinary daily life: distributed solar and storage economics; water budgeting, harvesting, reuse and drought-resilient landscaping; low-cost earthen and hybrid construction methods; sensor-based environmental monitoring built with privacy-preserving governance from the start; local food and controlled-environment agriculture experiments; participatory digital tools for transparent community accounting; and open documentation of performance data, failures and lessons learned. The commitment is to publish what did not work as readily as what did.

06 · Civic & Governance Prototype

Self-government, rooted rather than borrowed

The social experiment matters as much as the architecture. New Kashan embodies a small-scale model of accountable, pluralistic self-government — without becoming ideologically rigid, and without needing to import a governance vocabulary from anywhere but its own tradition.

"No governance by personality alone. Institutions should survive the founders."

Persian civic life has its own deep grammar of consultation and shared authority, distinct from — and older than — the imperial imagery the architecture deliberately avoids. The anjoman (elected local council) tradition runs through Iran's constitutional movements; shura, consultative assembly, is a civic instinct that predates any single regime; and the Cyrus Cylinder is still invoked, accurately or not, as an early articulation of tolerance for difference within one polity. The Zoroastrian ethical triad — good thoughts, good words, good deeds (Humata, Hukhta, Huvarshta) — offers a workable, non-sectarian civic ethic rather than a religious requirement: it asks what a resident intends, says and does, not what a resident believes. New Kashan's charter can draw on this vocabulary honestly, the same way its walls draw on khesht — as inheritance, not costume. That is what the charter is meant to produce in practice: not consensus by default, but a repeatable way for many hands to reach one decision together — the image this tab opens with.

Institutional design
  • A written village charter establishing rights, responsibilities, decision domains and limits of community authority
  • An elected or rotating council (a contemporary anjoman) with transparent budgets and published decisions
  • Clear separation between property ownership/investment interests and community-governance rights, where possible
  • Supermajority thresholds for constitutional issues; simple or consent-based process (shura-style consultation) for ordinary operational matters
  • An independent conflict-resolution and mediation process
  • Strong protections for privacy, personal autonomy, belief, speech and lifestyle within reasonable community-impact rules
  • A resident onboarding process that communicates expectations without requiring cultural or ideological conformity
  • Annual civic review — what worked, what failed, what should change, published openly
  • NO forced cultural conformity — Persian identity is the village's inspiration, not a test of belonging
  • NO requirement that residents be Iranian, or share a single political view of Iran's future
  • NO governance that depends on any one founder's continued presence
    • 07 · Cultural Life

    Persian because of what people do there

    Culture is practiced rather than displayed. The village becomes Persian through its rituals, its table, its music and its calendar — not through applied ornament. A working annual cycle of festivals gives the architecture — the majlis court, the caravanserai, the garden — something real to be used for.

    An annual festival calendar for New Kashan
    Weeks before spring equinox

    Chaharshanbe Suri

    The fire-jumping eve before Nowruz. In the village: bonfires in the majlis court, the old call-and-response of leaving winter's pallor behind, welcomed as a community-wide threshold ritual rather than a private household one.

    March 20–21 · Spring equinox

    Nowruz

    The Persian new year and the village's central civic-cultural event: a shared haft-sin at the caravanserai, thirteen days of open hospitality, and the annual charter review timed to fall within the season of renewal.

    13th day after Nowruz

    Sizdah Bedar

    "Getting rid of thirteen" — a day spent outdoors, sabzeh (the sprouted greens of the haft-sin) returned to running water or open land. A natural fit for the garden and the preserved desert landscape beyond the village edge.

    Late June / early July

    Tirgan

    The midsummer water festival, honoring the archer-hero Arash and the rains. Water-play at the garden's rills and basins, poetry and music through the night — a natural companion to the village's water-as-precious design ethic.

    Early October · Autumn equinox

    Mehregan

    The harvest festival of Mehr (friendship, covenant, and the sun). Feasting on the community's own kitchen-garden yield, and a natural moment for the residency and fellowship cohort to present a season's work.

    December (Zoroastrian calendar)

    Yalda Night

    The longest night of the year, spent awake together until dawn — pomegranates, watermelon, and the poetry of Hafez read aloud with a bibliomantic falling-open of the book. The retreat hall's obvious winter counterpart to Tirgan's summer water-play.

    The rest of cultural life
    • Poetry evenings spanning Hafez, Rumi, Forough Farrokhzad, contemporary Iranian voices and translated world poetry
    • Persian cooking, bread, tea and shared-table traditions as daily practice, not event programming
    • Music from classical Persian forms to contemporary Iranian, jazz, global and experimental collaboration
    • Craft workshops connecting traditional knowledge to contemporary design, not souvenir production
    • Artist, architect, scientist and civic-innovation residencies
    • Lectures and salons on Iranian history, ecology, democracy, technology and future reconstruction — bilingual where useful, with no requirement that residents be Iranian
    08 · Impact Startup Ecosystem

    A founding ground, not just a founders' village

    The living-laboratory work in the sustainability program (net-zero energy, water budgeting, earthen construction, participatory governance tools) generates real problems worth solving — and a small resident and visiting community of technical, civic and design talent already gathered to solve them. New Kashan can formalize that into the seed of an impact-startup ecosystem, without turning the village itself into a corporate campus.

    The village is the pilot customer, the diaspora is the network, and the problems are the ones the village already has to solve for itself.

    What the ecosystem is built from

    Residency → venture pipeline

    Fellows become founders

    • Short residencies (weeks) for exploration; longer fellowships (a season) for teams building a working prototype against a real village need
    • The village's own systems — microgrid economics, water metering, earthen-building methods, participatory governance software — become the first reference deployment for graduating ventures
    • A light seed fund or philanthropic pool for the strongest teams, sized to stay a village-scale instrument rather than a fund with its own gravity

    Diaspora & future-Iran network

    A bench, not a headquarters

    • Iranian and Iranian-diaspora founders, engineers, scientists and civic technologists as the anchor network — but never a requirement for participation
    • Demo days timed to Mehregan (harvest) and Nowruz (renewal), so pitch events sit inside the cultural calendar rather than beside it
    • Open documentation, shared with future rebuilders of Iranian infrastructure, whenever and however that reconstruction becomes possible
    Candidate focus areas — drawn directly from the living lab

    Energy & water

    Microgrid economics, arid-region water budgeting and reuse hardware, drought-resilient agtech.

    Construction

    Low-cost earthen and hybrid building methods, modular components for rapid, climate-appropriate rebuilding.

    Civic tech

    Participatory governance and transparent accounting tools, privacy-preserving community sensor networks.

    The economic model for the ecosystem should sit alongside — not inside — the hospitality and resident-dues model described under Retreats & Hospitality, likely as a separate nonprofit or program-related-investment vehicle so that venture returns, if any, don't create governance conflicts inside the village charter.

    09 · Retreats, Hospitality & Program

    A village that pays for its own commons

    The community should not depend entirely on resident dues. A diversified operating model keeps the common spaces alive and the village outward-facing, while retreats give visitors an honest, non-performative way to encounter the culture the village actually practices.

    The development program
    Permanent homes7–8 dwellings occupied year-round by founders/residents, ~700–1,400 sq ft depending on household.
    Flexible residencesCottages or courtyard units for part-time residents, fellows, visiting teachers and longer-stay guests.
    Guest / retreat lodgingA portion of the village operated as short-stay lodging when legally permitted, subsidizing common infrastructure.
    Caravanserai / village houseThe social heart — reception, tea room, communal dining, kitchen, lounge, library, co-working, meeting rooms, shaded courtyard.
    Retreat hallFlexible studio for yoga, movement, lectures, workshops, meditation, community assemblies and small performances.
    Bath / wellness houseA contemporary hammam — sauna/steam where feasible, treatment rooms, changing spaces, quiet courtyard.
    Garden + food landscapePersian garden geometry translated to desert ecology — orchard where viable, herbs, edible planting, greenhouse, community kitchen garden.
    Maker / craft spaceCeramics, textiles, woodworking, repair, and experimentation with earthen construction and appropriate technology.
    Amphitheater / majlis courtSmall outdoor performance and conversation space for poetry, music, film and civic gatherings — and the festival calendar above.
    Service edgeParking, deliveries, waste, maintenance and fire access kept at the perimeter so the village core stays pedestrian.
    Revenue & program mix
    • Short-stay lodging / vacation rentals where zoning and licensing allow
    • Curated retreats — design, wellness, culture, leadership, ecology and future-Iran convenings
    • Private group buyouts during selected periods
    • Food, tea and cultural programming for day visitors, if permitted
    • Residencies and fellowships funded by sponsors or philanthropy (feeding the startup ecosystem above)
    • Workshops and educational programs
    • Small weddings, celebrations and organizational off-sites, with strict scale and noise limits
    • A longer-term nonprofit cultural/educational arm alongside the property/operating entity
    10 · Land, Permits & Phasing

    Land follows the program — never the reverse

    This is the highest-risk part of the project and should drive land acquisition. Do not buy land first and try to force the program onto it afterward. A parcel should be screened by a New Mexico land-use attorney, civil engineer and architect before acquisition.

    Phasing
    Phase 0 — Feasibility

    Define, screen, verify

    Define program, ownership model and economics; identify 5–10 candidate parcels; complete zoning, water, wastewater, access, environmental and preliminary cost due diligence.

    Phase 1 — Seed Village

    Acquire, entitle, build the core

    Acquire land; entitle master plan; build infrastructure, the central village house/retreat hall and 6–8 dwellings. Establish the landscape framework and governance charter.

    Phase 2 — Living Community

    Expand and operate

    Expand to roughly 12–16 dwellings, wellness/craft spaces and mature hospitality operations. Begin formal living-lab research, residency and startup-ecosystem programming.

    Phase 3 — Full Village

    Grow only once proven

    Grow toward ~25 dwellings only after social, operational, ecological and financial systems have been tested. Preserve the ability to stop at a smaller, coherent village if that proves optimal.

    Likely entitlement / permit workstreams
    • Zoning determination — base zoning, density, overlays, allowed uses, before contingencies expire
    • Planned Development / rezoning — Santa Fe County's PD framework is built for coordinated, mixed-use plans like this one
    • Subdivision / land division, if creating separately owned lots
    • Conceptual/master plan + site development plan, especially given the phased approach
    • Development and building permits, including New Mexico's earthen-building code framework
    • Grading, drainage and terrain management for roads, pads and stormwater control
    • Water approvals — well permitting, Office of the State Engineer, water rights complexity
    • Wastewater / septic approvals for on-site or engineered systems
    • Fire and emergency access — road width, turnarounds, defensible space
    • Commercial / assembly / lodging approvals for the retreat hall, food service and events
    Design non-negotiables
    • NO Disneyland Persia — references must have functional, spatial or cultural integrity
    • NO scattered suburban subdivision — the project must read as a village
    • NO water fantasy — the landscape must honestly belong to an arid region
    • NO sustainability theater — measure performance, design for maintainability
    • NO car-dominated center — most parking and service movement stays at the edge
    • NO forced cultural conformity — Persian identity inspires, it does not gatekeep belonging
    • NO land purchase until density, use mix, water and wastewater have a credible entitlement path
    • NO requirement to build all 25 homes — coherence over maximum buildout
    Immediate next steps
    • Commission a one-day architect + land-use planner workshop to turn this brief into a quantified program and bubble/site diagrams
    • Define the legal/economic structure — privately held village, condominium/co-op, subdivision, land trust, or hybrid
    • Set a preliminary land budget and Phase 1 capital envelope
    • Engage a Santa Fe County land-use attorney or entitlement consultant before touring land seriously
    • Build a parcel screening scorecard — zoning/density, water, wastewater, access, airport/city time, views, solar, hazards, neighbors, expansion, price
    • Request a pre-application conversation with Santa Fe County Growth Management for each finalist parcel

    These notes are strategic, not legal advice. Santa Fe County's public guidance states that development outside incorporated municipalities must comply with the Sustainable Land Development Code and generally requires a County development permit; verify zoning, density, overlays and legal-lot status before development.
